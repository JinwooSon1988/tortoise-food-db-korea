# Evidence expansion 3 — radicchio, chicory, mulberry

## Radicchio
- Decision: `limited_supplement` for `Mediterranean_Testudo`.
- Evidence: Tortoise Trust winter-feeding guidance explicitly lists radicchio among supermarket leafy greens to rotate and mix when natural forage is limited.
- Scope limit: this is a winter/substitute mixed-green context, not evidence for staple or unlimited feeding, and not Ibera-specific dose evidence.
- Do not transfer the radicchio judgment automatically to all `Cichorium intybus` products.

## Chicory
- Current public verdict remains hold/unreviewed.
- The 2021 Food Chemistry review documents nutritional composition and food/feed use of `Cichorium intybus`, but it is not tortoise husbandry evidence.
- No sufficiently direct Mediterranean Testudo feeding source was verified in this batch, so the plant is not promoted merely because radicchio is a `Cichorium` cultivar.

## Mulberry leaf
- Current public verdict remains hold/unreviewed.
- Peer-reviewed animal-feed literature supports that `Morus` leaves have substantial nutritional value, but this evidence is largely livestock/rodent or composition-focused rather than Testudo feeding evidence.
- A 2025 composition study also shows material differences among multiple `Morus` species. Therefore Korean retail `뽕잎` should not be treated as one fixed botanical/nutrient record without species-level mapping.

## Safety rule retained
- Wild ingestion ≠ unlimited captive recommendation.
- Human/livestock food chemistry ≠ tortoise safety threshold.
- Related taxon ≠ same feeding verdict.
- Absence of evidence ≠ evidence of safety.
