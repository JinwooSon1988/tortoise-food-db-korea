Bell pepper

Capsicum annuum
DDo not feed

Do not include it in the planned diet. This is a separate question from whether an accidental small bite is toxic.

Why this assessmentA specialist tortoise plant database (The Tortoise Table) classifies sweet pepper fruit as do not feed. No direct feeding-safety trials in tortoises that would overturn this have been found. The study that analysed the chemical composition of Capsicum annuum fruit describes the composition of non-pungent fruit, but it is not evidence that the fruit is safe to feed to tortoises. Until more evidence is available, sweet peppers are therefore excluded from the planned diet. This does not mean that a single bite of sweet pepper causes poisoning, and it is not a claim that a toxic dose has been established.

Peer-reviewed paperComposition evidence
What the source says

The study directly characterized volatile and non-volatile chemistry in mature fresh sweet-pepper fruits across multiple Capsicum annuum genotypes. It supports treating sweet/bell pepper fruit as a distinct non-pungent food-type Capsicum concept rather than automatically transferring chemistry from hot chilli cultivars.

Role in this assessment
Indirect evidence that supplements the current conclusion. It does not decide safety on its own.

What this source cannot show
This is plant-composition and sensory research, not a tortoise feeding or toxicity trial. It does not establish that bell pepper is an optimal staple, a safe dose for Testudo, a fixed feeding frequency, or that every Capsicum annuum cultivar has identical chemistry.
